How to start a 5-minute family devotional with a 4-year-old

Skip the curriculum. One verse, one question, one song — and a routine that survives real mornings.

By the Kingdomland teamSeptember 1, 2026

Most family devotional plans are written for families who have already succeeded at family devotionals. They assume a quiet table, a child who sits, and twenty minutes nobody actually has at 7:10 a.m. If that isn't your house, this is for you.

The goal for a four-year-old is not depth. It is rhythm: a small, warm moment with God's Word that happens often enough to become "what we do." Here is the version that works in real homes.

The five-minute shape

  1. One verse (1 minute). Short — a single line. Say it, then say it again with a motion. "The Lord is my shepherd" with a hand on the heart is plenty.
  2. One question (2 minutes). Not "what does this mean?" but "who takes care of you?" Let them answer with whatever comes out. Nod. That's theology at four.
  3. One song (2 minutes). Sing the verse or play a memory verse track and let them dance. Music does the memorizing so you don't have to.

That's it. Same verse all week. By Friday they will beat you to the second half.

When they wiggle

They will. Wiggling is not disrespect; it is a four-year-old's body doing its job. Move the devotional to where the wiggling already happens: the car seat, the bath, the walk to daycare. Scripture in the car on Tuesday counts exactly as much as Scripture at the table on Sunday.

“These words that I command you today shall be on your heart… talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way.” — Deuteronomy 6:6–7

Three verses to start with

  • Psalm 23:1 — "The Lord is my shepherd." Pairs with our Bible Adventures episode on Psalm 23.
  • Galatians 5:22 — "The fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace…" Take one fruit a week; here's a kid-sized guide.
  • Genesis 1:1 — "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth." Perfect after a Creation episode.

Let the screen help, once

A four-minute episode can be the question. Watch one Marvelous Light story, pause at the end, and ask: "What did Lucy learn?" You have just done a devotional without opening a workbook. Our memory verse tracks exist for exactly this — press play, and the verse follows your child around the house.
