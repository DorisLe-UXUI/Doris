The fruits of the Spirit for kids: a parent's plain-English guide

All nine, in words a six-year-old gets — with one thing to try for each.

By the Kingdomland teamAugust 20, 2026

Galatians 5:22–23 gives us a list: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. Nine words, most of them abstract. Here's how we explain each one to the kids in Marvelous Light — and one thing your child can try this week.

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control.” — Galatians 5:22–23

First, why "fruit"?

Because fruit grows. You don't make an apple by trying hard; you stay attached to the tree. Tell your child: these are what grows in us when we stay close to Jesus. That takes the pressure off "being good" and puts it where Paul put it — on being connected.

The nine, kid-sized

  • Love — wanting good things for someone even when they're annoying. Try: say one kind thing to a sibling before breakfast.
  • Joy — a happy that stays even on a rotten day, because God is still God. Try: name one good thing at bedtime after a hard day. (Yes, our episode is literally called "A Rotten Day.")
  • Peace — a calm heart when things are loud. Try: three slow breaths and "God is with me" before a scary thing.
  • Patience — waiting without whining. Try: wait for the cookie until everyone has one.
  • Kindness — doing something nice on purpose. Try: hold the door for someone.
  • Goodness — doing the right thing when nobody is looking. Try: tell the truth about the broken thing.
  • Faithfulness — keeping your promise. Try: finish the chore you said you'd do.
  • Gentleness — being strong softly. Try: use a quiet voice with the baby or the dog.
  • Self-control — being the boss of your body. Try: stop when Mom says stop, the first time.

A memory trick

Group them in threes: love, joy, peace (how I feel inside), patience, kindness, goodness (how I treat people), faithfulness, gentleness, self-control (how I act when it's hard). Three fingers, three times. Most kids have all nine in a week.
