What to look for in a Christian show for kids: a 6-point checklist

Not every "faith-based" label means the same thing. Six questions to ask before you press play.

By the Kingdomland teamSeptember 8, 2026

Search "Christian shows for kids" and you'll find hundreds. Some are wonderful. Some are a cartoon with a Bible verse stapled to the end. Here's the checklist we use when we decide what belongs on Kingdomland — and what we'd ask of any show, including ours.

1. Does it tell the Bible story, or just borrow the moral?

"Be kind" is nice. "Zacchaeus climbed a tree to see Jesus, and Jesus called him by name" is Scripture. Look for shows where kids meet the actual people and events of the Bible, so the lesson has roots.

2. What plays when the episode ends?

On ad-supported platforms, the show is only part of the experience. Ads, autoplay and recommendations are the rest — and none of them were chosen by you. A closed, ad-free library means "next" is always something you'd approve of.

3. Do your kids see themselves in it?

Children learn who belongs in God's story by who they see in it. Look for casts, voices and families that look like yours. For our families, that means African American voices across every series — not one character in one episode.

4. Is it paced for the age on the label?

A four-year-old's attention runs in short arcs. Great preschool shows keep episodes short, repeat the key idea, and end with something a child can say back to you. If you can't summarize the episode in one sentence, neither can they.

5. What happens after the screen goes dark?

The best shows give you a next step: a verse to sing, a question to ask at dinner, a quiz to play together. Watching becomes remembering. That's the difference between screen time and screen time that gives back.

6. Can you try it before you pay, and leave when you want?

A show worth paying for should let you preview it and cancel in a click. Be wary of anything that hides the price, the trial terms or the cancel button.
